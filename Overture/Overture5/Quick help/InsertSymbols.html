If Tools Panel is not showing click on the "Score Tools" button at top left of Control Bar or type  "Shift-T" and then click on the Symbols section in the header if it's not active. You can also type "Ctrl[cmd]-Shift-S" to go directly to the Symbols section.


  1. Select the symbol from the appropriate palette, Your cursor becomes a cross hair.
  2. Click on the score to enter the symbol. Some symbols like "hairpins" require you to drag to set it's end point.


The Symbol panel has a number of palettes whose tools are called symbols. These are the ornaments, articulations, note heads, dynamics, expressions, text and clefs palettes. This section introduces you to the basics of placing and moving symbols on the score.


Some symbols are contained within a measure like note, dynamics, etc. and some are attached to note like articulations and ornaments.


For symbols that are contained in measures:


  1. Select the symbol from the appropriate palette, Your cursor becomes a cross hair.
  2. Click on the score to enter the symbol. Some symbols like "hairpins" require you to drag to set it's end point.


Some symbols, like the crescendo hairpin, are re-sizable. To see how this works, add a crescendo and diminuendo to the score.

  1. Choose the crescendo hairpin from the dynamics palette.
  2. Put the pointer on the score, press and hold the mouse button and drag to the right. The crescendo symbol appears and grows as you drag.


For symbols that are attached to a note:


  1. Select the symbol from the appropriate palette, Your cursor becomes a cross hair.
  2. Click on the note to attach the symbol to a note.
    To add the same ornament or articulation to many notes, drag a box around them with the ornament tool.
  3. To change how the symbol affects the notes playback, double click on it to open it's playback dialog.
  4. You can drag an attached symbol wherever you please, but it remains attached to the original note for playback purposes.


Note: You can enter symbols directly on the score not attached to any note by using the same method for symbols contained in a measure.
